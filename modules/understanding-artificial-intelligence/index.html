MODULE OVERVIEW

From AI Concepts to Learning Systems

This module develops the foundations required to understand Artificial Intelligence beyond its business applications. It progresses from core AI concepts and intelligent systems into machine learning, deep learning, feature engineering, supervised and unsupervised learning, model evaluation, and practical AI solution development.

AI Foundations

Understanding Artificial Intelligence concepts, intelligent systems, learning approaches, and the development of AI as a discipline.

Machine Learning

Developing an understanding of supervised and unsupervised learning, model training, algorithms, and the role of data in learning systems.

Practical Development

Building familiarity with Python, Jupyter Notebook, Scikit-learn, PyTorch, data preparation, experimentation, and model evaluation.

LEARNING JOURNEY

Key Learning Themes

Rather than documenting every unit separately, the learning journey is organised around the major capabilities developed throughout the module.

FOUNDATIONS

Understanding AI Systems

Exploring what constitutes an Artificial Intelligence system, how learning systems differ from conventional software, and how different AI approaches are selected for different types of problems.

LEARNING APPROACHES

Machine Learning & Deep Learning

Developing an understanding of supervised and unsupervised learning, machine learning algorithms, deep learning concepts, model training, and the importance of data.

DATA

Feature Engineering & Representation

Understanding how data is selected, transformed, represented, and prepared so that learning algorithms can identify meaningful patterns.

SUPERVISED LEARNING

Classification & Regression

Applying structured approaches such as CRISP-DM to explore data and understand how supervised learning solutions are developed for prediction and classification problems.

UNSUPERVISED LEARNING

Clustering

Exploring how learning systems can identify patterns and groups within data without predefined target labels, including the practical development of clustering solutions.

EVALUATION

Model Performance

Examining model evaluation approaches including validation strategies, accuracy, precision, recall, F1 score, sensitivity, specificity, and the importance of selecting metrics appropriate to the problem.

TECHNICAL DEVELOPMENT

Building Practical AI Capability

A major development goal within this module is moving from conceptual familiarity with AI toward greater confidence with the technical processes behind machine learning systems.

Python

Continued development of Python fundamentals including lists, strings, functions, data handling, and computational problem solving.

Jupyter Notebook

Using notebook-based workflows to explore datasets, execute machine learning experiments, document analysis, and interpret results.

Scikit-learn & PyTorch

Building familiarity with machine learning and deep learning frameworks used to prepare data, train models, and evaluate learning systems.

LEARNING EVIDENCE · CLD2

Collaborative Learning Discussion 2

The second Collaborative Learning Discussion built on the technical themes developed throughout the module. It provided an opportunity to consider how different Artificial Intelligence and machine learning approaches can be evaluated and selected in relation to the problem being addressed.

TECHNICAL UNDERSTANDING

Choosing an Appropriate Approach

A key learning point was that selecting an AI technique should begin with the characteristics of the problem and the available data rather than with a preferred technology. Different learning approaches involve different assumptions, requirements, strengths, and limitations.

EVALUATION

Looking Beyond Accuracy

The discussion strengthened my understanding that model performance cannot always be represented by a single metric. Measures such as precision, recall, F1 score, sensitivity, and specificity provide different perspectives on performance, and their relevance depends on the consequences of different types of error.

PROFESSIONAL CONNECTION

Better Questions for AI Delivery

Developing greater technical understanding changes the questions I can ask when working with AI teams. Rather than focusing only on whether a model performs well, I can increasingly examine how performance is measured, why a particular approach was selected, what trade-offs exist, and whether the evidence supports the intended business use.

Assessment Outcome

70% · Distinction

Achieving a Distinction in the second Collaborative Learning Discussion provided evidence of continued academic development while the technical content of the module was becoming more demanding. The activity also reinforced the connection between technical understanding and informed professional judgement.

LEARNING EVIDENCE · INDIVIDUAL ESSAY

Artificial Intelligence and Its Applications

The individual essay required the development of a management-focused report comparing traditional machine learning, deep learning, and Large Language Model frameworks to inform AI investment decisions. I positioned the analysis within pharmaceutical distribution, linking the technical comparison to practical technology selection and enterprise decision-making.

Assessment Outcome · 40% of Module

68% · Merit

The report was assessed as clear, well organised, technically accurate, and accessible to a management audience. Its central argument was that machine learning, deep learning, and LLMs should be viewed as complementary technologies, with organisations selecting the least complex approach capable of meeting the requirement.

WHAT WORKED WELL

Strengths Identified Through Feedback

Conceptual Clarity

The relationship between machine learning, deep learning, and Large Language Models was explained clearly and accurately. Rather than presenting them as competing technologies, the report positioned them as related approaches with different levels of complexity and suitability.

Business-Centred Argument

The report framed technology selection around the incremental value generated by additional complexity. This created a management-relevant decision principle rather than treating technical sophistication as an objective in itself.

Communication & Structure

The structure and presentation achieved the strongest marks. The report was considered logical, fluent, and accessible to non-specialists, demonstrating an ability to translate technical AI concepts for a management audience.

DEVELOPMENT AREAS

What Prevented the Work Reaching Distinction

CRITICALITY

Test Claims, Not Just State Them

The most important development area was critical analysis. Although the central argument was coherent, it was repeated more often than it was challenged with evidence. Claims about the relative performance or suitability of different AI approaches require stronger empirical support and consideration of the circumstances in which those claims may not hold.

TECHNICAL DEPTH

Understand How Models Are Trained

The technical comparison needed greater depth around training. This includes gradient-based neural network training and a clearer distinction between LLM pre-training, fine-tuning, instruction tuning, reinforcement learning from human feedback, prompting, and retrieval-augmented generation.

EVIDENCE

Broaden the Research Base

The sources used were relevant and correctly applied, but the evidence base was relatively narrow. Several application and comparative claims were not directly supported by academic evidence. Future work needs broader sourcing and tighter links between claims and supporting research.

RECOMMENDATIONS

Move from Principle to Decision

The final recommendation needed to go beyond a general technology selection principle. A stronger management recommendation would prioritise a first use case and define required data, success measures, indicative costs, and a phased implementation approach.

CONTEXT

Make Regulation Specific

The pharmaceutical context was relevant but needed greater regulatory specificity. Future analysis should explicitly examine applicable frameworks such as Good Distribution Practice, data protection requirements, and the EU AI Act when assessing how an AI solution can be deployed and governed.

EXAMPLES

Develop Technical Examples

Examples such as regression, CNNs, RNNs, and GPT-based approaches should be analysed rather than simply named. This includes explaining their operation, limitations, and suitability for particular problem types.

REFLECTION

Turning Feedback into Development

Key Learning

Strong AI leadership requires being able to challenge the technical argument, not only communicate it.

The feedback highlighted a useful distinction in my current capability. I am already comfortable framing AI decisions for management, connecting technology to business value, and communicating complex topics clearly. The next stage of my development is to strengthen the technical and analytical depth underneath those decisions.

In particular, I need to move beyond knowing what different approaches are used for and develop a stronger understanding of how they are trained, why they behave differently, how their performance should be evaluated, and what evidence supports choosing one approach over another.

ACTION PLAN

Applying the Feedback to the Final Assessment

1. Increase Technical Depth

Explain model selection, training, data preparation, and evaluation decisions rather than only describing the resulting solution.

2. Strengthen Critical Analysis

Support important claims with evidence, compare alternatives, discuss limitations, and explain why the selected approach is appropriate.

3. Evaluate with Purpose

Select evaluation measures based on the problem and explain what the results mean rather than reporting performance metrics without interpretation.

4. Make Recommendations Actionable

Translate technical findings into prioritised recommendations with clear success criteria, constraints, risks, and next steps.
